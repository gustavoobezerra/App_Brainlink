## 1. ASRS screener accuracy

### 1.1 Brevik EJ et al. 2020, Brain Behav 10(6):e01605 — [FULL TEXT] PMC7303368, PMID 32285644, doi:10.1002/brb3.1605
- Norway. n=646 clinically diagnosed adult ADHD (registry, expert-committee approved ICD-10/DSM-IV-TR dx) vs n=908 population controls (Medical Birth Registry, 18-40 y). Total 1554.
- Ages: ADHD 34.0 (10.3); controls 29.4 (7.8). Females 48.5% ADHD vs 59.9% controls.
- ASRS-18 scored 0-4 per item (0-72 total). ASRS means: controls 23.0 (9.8); ADHD 45.0 (12.6); on meds 43.5 (13.3); off meds 48.1 (10.4).
- AUC: ASRS-18 0.904 (95% CI 0.888-0.921); ASRS short screener (6-item sum) 0.903 (0.886-0.920); WURS 0.956 (0.946-0.965); combined 0.964.
- Cronbach alpha full ASRS 0.952; Inattentive factor 0.924; H/I factor 0.918. Two-factor PCA (62.2% variance).
- Cutoffs for ASRS-18 (0-72): sens .95 -> cutoff 21, spec .45; sens .90 -> 27, spec .71; sens .80 -> 35, spec .88; spec .95 -> 42, sens .64; spec .90 -> 36, sens .79.
- Authors: "ASRS is not adequate in situations requiring very high sensitivity, as specificity was merely 0.45 at sensitivity 0.95."
- Limitations: ASRS may be affected by affective fluctuations, time of day, sleep problems. Case-control design (diagnosed vs population) inflates AUC vs real screening contexts ("spectrum bias" — my note).
- NOTE: Brevik did NOT report the 0-24 cutoff-14 performance in the text; they used 6-item sum and "dichotomous" in figure only.

### 1.2 Kessler RC et al. 2005, Psychol Med 35(2):245-256 — [ABSTRACT] PMID 15841682, doi:10.1017/s0033291704002892
- ASRS = 18 DSM-IV Criterion A symptom questions; screener = 6 of 18 chosen by stepwise logistic regression.
- Calibration: n=154 NCS-R respondents, oversampling those reporting childhood ADHD + adult persistence; blind clinical ratings.
- Symptom-level concordance kappa 0.16-0.81.
- Unweighted 6-item screener (DICHOTOMOUS scoring, i.e. shaded-box threshold) vs unweighted 18-item: sensitivity 68.7% vs 56.3%; specificity 99.5% vs 98.3%; total classification accuracy 97.9% vs 96.2%; kappa 0.76 vs 0.58.
- KEY: the 2005 paper validated the dichotomous ">=4 of 6 in shaded zone" scoring, NOT the 0-24 sum >=14.

### 1.3 Kessler RC et al. 2007, Int J Methods Psychiatr Res 16(2):52-65 — [ABSTRACT; PMC2044504 is PDF-only scan, could not extract] PMID 17623385, doi:10.1002/mpr.208
- US health-plan subscribers, convenience subsample n=668; screener given twice (test-retest) then 3rd time with clinical interviewer (DSM-IV adult ADHD). Data weighted.
- Internal consistency of continuous screener 0.63-0.72; test-retest Pearson r 0.58-0.77.
- "A four-category version" (scored 0-24 and grouped 0-9, 10-13, 14-17, 18-24 per secondary sources; per WebFetch of PMC page) had AUC 0.90 vs clinician dx.
- This is the source of the 0-24 continuous scoring the app uses (cut 14 = start of 3rd stratum). Stratum-specific prevalence/PPV not extracted (full text inaccessible) -> DO NOT quote sens/spec for >=14 from this paper without reading it.

### 1.4 Costa IM, Ballalai MES, Maia LM, Taunay TCD. 2026, Appl Neuropsychol Adult, online 22 Sep 2026, pp.1-11 — [ABSTRACT; T&F full text blocked 403] PMID 42771540, doi:10.1080/23279095.2026.2735472 (Univ. Fortaleza)
- n=805 Brazilian adults with and without a *self-reported previous* ADHD diagnosis (criterion = reported dx, not clinical interview).
- CFA two-factor: CFI .929, TLI .919, SRMR .060, RMSEA .085 (slightly above).
- Invariance configural/metric/scalar across reported-dx groups. Omega .90 (IN) and .87 (HI).
- Effect sizes Inattention = .56, H/I = .50 (abstract symbol lost; "large effects").
- AUC: total .83; Inattention .83; six-item Part A screener .82.
- "Positive predictive values fell under realistic base rates, so a positive result indicates further assessment; cutoffs were stable but remain preliminary and sample-specific."
- Convergent: correlated with anxiety, depression, perceived stress, impulsive buying.

- Mattos P et al. 2024. J Atten Disord 28(7):1082-1091 — [ABSTRACT] PMID 38380531, doi:10.1177/10870547241229097. Rio de Janeiro household survey, n=2297 aged 19-60, lay interviewers, ASRS-6. Prevalence 4.59% (95% CI 3.56-5.44).
- Lewczuk K et al. 2024. J Atten Disord 28(4):512-530 — [ABSTRACT] doi:10.1177/10870547231215518. 42 countries, N=72,627; ASRS screener "at risk" 21.4% women, 18.1% men; partial invariance; highlights low specificity of screeners.

**Synthesis on Brazilian cutoffs:** Brazilian literature uses (a) ASRS-18 with DSM-like symptom count (>=5 or >=6 symptoms "often/very often" per domain; Mattos 2018; Di Santi 2025) or (b) ASRS-6 with Kessler 2005 dichotomous rule (>=4 shaded boxes; Ferreira 2025, Di Santi 2025). I found NO Brazilian study validating the 0-24 sum with cut >=14 against clinical interview. Costa 2026 reports Part A AUC .82 but cutoffs "preliminary and sample-specific"; criterion was self-reported dx. No Brazilian study validating ASRS-6 against interview in university students found (Mattos 2018 used ASRS-18 in medical students).

## 5. Prevalence (for power calculations)
- Song P et al. 2021. J Glob Health 11:04009 — [FULL TEXT] PMC7916320, doi:10.7189/jogh.11.04009. Persistent adult ADHD pooled 4.61% (95% CI 3.41-5.99; 20 articles, 107,282); HIC 3.25% vs LMIC 8.00% (3.79-13.57). Symptomatic adult ADHD pooled 8.83% (7.23-10.57). Age-standardised 2020: persistent 2.58% (1.51-4.45), symptomatic 6.76% (4.31-10.61). Age 18-24: persistent 5.05% (3.12-8.07); symptomatic 8.99% (6.12-13.03). Subgroup by tool (symptomatic): ASRS-6 8.04% (6.61-9.61); ASRS-18 10.1% (5.73-15.53).
- Brazil: Polanczyk 2010 ASRS screener+ 5.8% (4.8-7.0) household n=3007; Mattos 2024 Rio 4.59% (3.56-5.44) n=2297 ASRS-6; Mattos 2018 medical students: ASRS-18 positive 37%, interview dx 7.9%, after probing 4.5%, projected 4.1%; Ferreira 2025 medical students ASRS-6 >=4: 40.4% (n=99, excluded diagnosed).
- University (international): WMH-ICS screen+ 15.9% (DSM-IV wave), 8.1% (DSM-5 wave).
- PLANNING NUMBERS (my synthesis): for an unselected UNIPAC student sample expect ~15-40% ASRS-6 screen-positive depending on scoring (>=4 dichotomous gives higher rates than >=14 sum) and ~4-8% true clinical ADHD. Screen-positive proportion (~20-35%) is what matters for group-comparison power; true-case prevalence matters for PPV messaging.

## 2. Objective attention tasks feasible on a smartphone

### 2.1 Bella-Fernández M, Martin-Moratinos M, Li C, Wang P, Blasco-Fontecilla H. 2024 (online 2023). Neuropsychol Rev 34(1):320-337 — [FULL TEXT] PMC10920450, PMID 36877328, doi:10.1007/s11065-023-09587-2
- Meta-analysis, 51 studies, 139 outcomes (multilevel metafor, clustered by study/author).
- Pooled ADHD vs control: mu d=0.045 (95% CI -0.040, 0.129) n.s.; sigma d=0.255 (0.144, 0.366); tau d=0.529 (0.399, 0.660).
- Tau by domain: sustained attention 0.62 (0.49-0.76) [largest]; inhibitory control 0.53; selective attention 0.49; working memory 0.46; time perception 0.42; cognitive flexibility 0.37 (one outcome).
- Sigma by domain: sustained attention 0.31 (0.17-0.44); time perception 0.45.
- Age NOT a significant moderator of mu/sigma/tau -> differences "seem to remain constant across developmental periods" (relevant for adults).
- ISI: quadratic relation with tau significant (state-regulation-like). Medication status little impact.
- Publication bias: funnel asymmetry significant for mu only.
- Interpretation: larger tau = more frequent/pronounced attentional lapses (one interpretation among several).
- Implication for app: a ~5-10 min smartphone sustained-attention/Go-NoGo task -> fit ex-Gaussian (needs ~100+ RTs per condition) -> tau is the most promising RT marker, but group effect is moderate (d~0.5) = heavy overlap; not diagnostic for individuals.

### 2.2 Ulberstad F, Boström H, Chavanon M-L, Knollmann M, Wiley J, Christiansen H, Thorell LB. 2020. Int J Methods Psychiatr Res 29(2):e1822 — [FULL TEXT] PMC7301281, PMID 32100383, doi:10.1002/mpr.1822
- QbCheck = web version of QbTest using laptop webcam for motion + space bar. Adolescent/adult version: 4 stimuli (red/blue circle/square), one-back identical-pairs target rule, stimuli 200 ms, SOA 2 s, 600 stimuli, 25% targets (=~20 min).
- Study I test-retest n=25 (11 ADHD, 14 controls; age 12-59): ICC (two-way mixed, absolute agreement) Microevents 0.90; Omission 0.84; Commission 0.82; RT 0.96; RT variability 0.88. Webcam vs infrared Microevents r=.91.
- Study II n=142 (69 ADHD, mean age 27.58; 73 controls, mean age 26.16; age 12-60): logistic regression 81.0% correct, sensitivity 82.6%, specificity 79.5%; AUC Microevent 0.82, RT variation 0.81, omissions 0.76, commissions 0.75, RT 0.73.
- Usability: 149 initiated, 142 completed; 24% reported some technical problem (30.4% ADHD vs 16.1% controls, p=.06). User-friendliness means >=8.06/10.
- Caveats: case-control (diagnosed vs healthy) -> inflated accuracy; manufacturer-affiliated authors (Ulberstad, Boström Qbtech).

### 2.3 Tomlinson E, Ward M, Walker J, et al., Whiting P. 2025. Health Technol Assess 29(58):1-216 — [ABSTRACT + plain-language summary; PMC12668258; full monograph on NCBI Bookshelf NBK618982 / NIHR library blocked by captcha] doi:10.3310/DRDR7171. PROSPERO CRD42023482963.

### 2.4 Chu K-C, Lu H-K, Huang M-C, Lin S-J, Liu W-I, Huang Y-S, Hsu J-F, Wang C-H. 2020. JMIR Ment Health 7(6):e12158 — [FULL TEXT] PMC7351267, PMID 32558658, doi:10.2196/12158
- CHILDREN 6-12 y (not adults), Taiwan, Chang Gung IRB. n=63: 49 ADHD (40 boys, 9 girls) vs 14 controls (5 boys, 9 girls) — unmatched sex/size (acknowledged).
- NeuroSky MindSet proprietary eSense "attention"/"meditation" + MotionWatch 8 actigraph; 3 x 20-min stages (pre, during CPT II, post).
- EEG attention correlated with hit RT ISI change r=-0.368 (P=.003) and hit SE ISI change r=-0.336 (P=.007). Actigraph correlated with confidence index r=0.352, omissions r=0.322, hit RT SE r=0.393, variability r=0.351. Group difference in activity P<.001 (between-subject P=.04). ### 2.5 Mind wandering (MW), SART, thought probes, ADHD
- CITATION CORRECTION: the ADHD paper by Seli is Seli P, Smallwood J, Cheyne JA, Smilek D. 2015. "On the relation of mind wandering and ADHD symptomatology." Psychon Bull Rev 22(3):629-636, doi:10.3758/s13423-014-0793-0 — [ABSTRACT]. (Not Psychol Sci; "Mind-wandering as a natural kind: a family-resemblances view" is a separate Seli et al. 2018 Trends Cogn Sci paper — not retrieved here.) Two non-clinical samples (Ns = 1,354) + clinically diagnosed ADHD sample: spontaneous (not deliberate) MW is central to ADHD symptomatology at clinical and non-clinical levels. Uses MW-D/MW-S scales (Carriere et al. 2013).
- Mowlem FD, Skirrow C, Reid P, Maltezos S, Nijjar SK, Merwood A, ... Asherson P. 2019. J Atten Disord 23(6):624-634 — [FULL TEXT] PMC6429624, doi:10.1177/1087054716651927.
  - MEWS: 15 items, 0-3 (0 = not at all/rarely ... 3 = nearly all the time/constantly). Copyrighted, free from corresponding author for research. Recommend excluding items 6, 10, 14 (coping items) -> 12-item MEWS.
  - Study 1 (MIRIAD; men; 41 ADHD vs 47 controls; ages 18-65): one factor 69.16% variance; alpha >.78; test-retest r=.84 (95% CI .74-.92) over mean 9.7 months; AUC .92 (.85-1.00).
  - Study 2 (OCEAN; 81 ADHD vs 30 controls): one factor 62.92%; test-retest r=.63 (.42-.80) over 6.4 months; AUC .96 (.93-.99); score 15 -> sensitivity .90, specificity .90. MEWS means: ADHD 27.72 (9.31) vs controls 7.21 (6.26).
  - Polyserial r with inattention .77-.81, H/I .69-.77, impairment .81-.82.
  - Authors: "we do not currently recommend the routine use of the MEWS to identify patients with ADHD until the scale has been comprehensively evaluated in other psychiatric disorders" (e.g., depressive rumination).
- Bozhilova NS, Michelini G, Kuntsi J, Asherson P. 2018. Neurosci Biobehav Rev 92:464-476 — [FULL TEXT] PMC6525148, doi:10.1016/j.neubiorev.2018.07.010. Hypothesis: deficient DMN regulation -> excessive spontaneous MW -> ADHD symptoms/impairment. MW measurable via rating scales, experience sampling in daily life and in experimental paradigms (probes), neural correlates. EEG: P1 attenuated during task-unrelated thought (perceptual decoupling); P3, N400, MMN reduced during MW. Deficient context regulation of EEG from rest to task in ADHD (Rommel 2016; Skirrow). Motivation/interest lower MW in SART (Seli 2015, 2016). Notes Shaw & Giambra 1993: more task-unrelated thoughts in college students with childhood ADHD history.
- Arabacı G, Parris BA. 2018. Sci Rep 8:4113 — [FULL TEXT] PMC5841345, doi:10.1038/s41598-018-22390-x. n=80 undiagnosed adults (mainly Bournemouth students). CAARS-S:S. SART: digits 1-9, 250 ms + 900 ms mask (1150 ms trial), no-go = 3, 900 trials + 20 probes (one random probe per 45 trials), probe "Which ... best characterises your mental state RIGHT NOW?" (on task / intentionally MW / unintentionally MW); easy version = sequential digits. Inattention predicted spontaneous MW in difficult SART (F(1,78)=13.14); spontaneous MW correlated with inattention r=.38 and hyperactivity r=.34 (difficult). Impulsivity negatively related to spontaneous MW (unexpected). -> TEMPLATE PROTOCOL for Study C (~17-18 min).
- Yeo LT, Fernando E, Brosowsky NP. 2026. Psychol Res 90(4) — [ABSTRACT] doi:10.1007/s00426-026-02358-7. SART with probes distinguishing remembered MW content vs "subjective feeling of inattention": feeling of inattention associated with inattentive symptoms, well-being, impairment, poorer SART; remembered MW mainly with H/I symptoms. -> probe wording matters; include both probe types.
- Kam JWY, Rahnuma T, Park YE, Hart CM. 2022. NeuroImage 258:119372 — [ABSTRACT] doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2022.119372. Systematic review, 42 EEG studies: most reliable markers = reduced P1, N1, P3 amplitudes during MW; spectral findings "less robust" but point to greater delta/theta/alpha and decreased beta during MW vs on-task; variability modulated by task context. -> spectral changes are small/inconsistent; single forehead channel cannot measure occipital alpha or ERPs well.
- Riby LM, Marr L, Barron-Millar L, Greer J, Hamilton CJ, McGann D, Smallwood J. 2025. J Integr Neurosci 24(3):26508 — [ABSTRACT] doi:10.31083/JIN26508. n=24 adults, vertical EOG, 3-stimulus oddball, DSSQ retrospective TUT: higher eye-blink rate associated with higher TUT; EBR and mu-RT predicted TUT; tau RT correlated with blink variability. -> supports blink rate as candidate MW correlate (small n).
- Unsworth N, Robison MK, Miller AL. 2019. Cogn Affect Behav Neurosci 19(4):1074-1093 — [ABSTRACT] doi:10.3758/s13415-019-00709-z. 5-min fixation baseline: "the vast majority of correlations with the baseline eye measures [incl. spontaneous blink rate] were weak and nonsignificant", including with ADHD symptom and MW questionnaires. -> trait-level blink-rate ~ ASRS correlation may be null; within-person state effects are the better bet.

## 3. Reliability study design (ICC, sample size, Bland-Altman)

### 3.1 Koo TK, Li MY. 2016. J Chiropr Med 15(2):155-163 — [ABSTRACT; PMC4913118 body not retrievable (captcha / empty)] PMID 27330520, doi:10.1016/j.jcm.2016.02.012
- 10 forms of ICC; must report software, "model", "type", "definition".
- Interpretation based on the 95% CI of the ICC: <0.5 poor; 0.5-0.75 moderate; 0.75-0.9 good; >0.90 excellent.
- (Commonly cited from the full text: test-retest -> two-way mixed effects, absolute agreement, single measurement; rule of thumb >=30 heterogeneous samples. NOT VERIFIED HERE -> present as standard practice, cite McGraw & Wong 1996 / Shrout & Fleiss 1979 if needed.)

### 3.2 Walter SD, Eliasziw M, Donner A. 1998. Stat Med 17(1):101-110 — [ABSTRACT] PMID 9463853, doi:10.1002/(sici)1097-0258(19980115)17:1<101::aid-sim727>3.0.co;2-e
- Approximation for number of subjects k to test H0: rho = rho0 vs rho1; "for reliability values of about 40 per cent or higher, use of two or three observations per subject will minimize the total number of observations required."
- Formula used (standard form of their approximation): k = 1 + 2 (z_alpha + z_beta)^2 n / [ (ln C0)^2 (n-1) ], C0 = (1 + n theta0)/(1 + n theta1), theta = rho/(1-rho).
- MY COMPUTATIONS (alpha .05 one-sided, power .80), n = 2 sessions: rho0 .5 vs rho1 .75 -> 36 subjects; .6 vs .8 -> 39; .5 vs .7 -> 63; .7 vs .85 -> 42; .4 vs .7 -> 33; .5 vs .8 -> 22. With n = 3 sessions: .5 vs .75 -> 24; .6 vs .8 -> 27.
- VERIFIED by Monte Carlo (one-way ICC F-test, 4000 reps): n=36 for .5 vs .75 -> power 0.806; n=39 for .6 vs .8 -> power 0.805.

### 3.3 Bonett DG. 2002. Stat Med 21(9):1331-1335 — [ABSTRACT] PMID 12111881, doi:10.1002/sim.1108
- Sample size for a CI of desired width for ICC in one-way and two-way ANOVA models.
- Formula used: n = 8 z^2 (1-rho)^2 (1+(k-1)rho)^2 / [k (k-1) w^2] + 1 (w = full CI width).
- MY COMPUTATIONS (95% CI, k = 2 sessions): planned rho .75 -> n = 75 for w = .20 (+/-.10); n = 34 for w = .30 (+/-.15). rho .70 -> 101 (w .20) / 46 (w .30). rho .80 -> 51 / 24. rho .60 -> 159 / 71. k = 3 sessions, w .20: rho .75 -> 51; rho .70 -> 68.
- VERIFIED by simulation: rho .75, n = 75, k = 2 -> mean CI width 0.204; n = 40 -> 0.286; n = 30 -> 0.335.

### 3.4 Bland-Altman
- Bland JM, Altman DG. 1986. Statistical methods for assessing agreement between two methods of clinical measurement. Lancet 1(8476):307-310 — [METADATA ONLY, PMID 2868172; PubMed lists no DOI].
- For test-retest of EEG features: plot (session2 - session1) vs mean; bias = mean difference; 95% limits of agreement = bias +/- 1.96 SD_diff; check proportional bias (regress diff on mean), log-transform power if heteroscedastic. Complement with SEM = SD x sqrt(1-ICC) and smallest detectable change SDC95 = 1.96 x sqrt(2) x SEM (standard psychometric formulas).

### 3.5 EEG test-retest benchmarks (for setting expected ICC)
- Rogers JM, Johnstone SJ, Aminov A, Donnelly J, Wilson PH. 2016. Test-retest reliability of a single-channel, wireless EEG system. ## 7. THREE CANDIDATE JOINT STUDIES (easiest -> most ambitious)

### STUDY A — "Entendi meu resultado?" Usability & comprehension of the ASRS screening report (lowest risk, ~1 semester)
- RQ: Do Brazilian university students correctly understand that the app's ASRS report indicates "possibilidade aumentada" and "NÃO é diagnóstico", that EEG does not change the result, and what to do next? Does an improved report (natural frequencies / icon array + explicit next steps) improve comprehension vs the current report without increasing distress?
- Design: two phases. A1 = randomized vignette experiment, 2 (report version: current vs improved) x 2 (vignette score: 16/24 "acima do ponto de corte" vs 8/24 "abaixo"), between-subjects; participants read a FICTIONAL person's report (no own screening -> minimal risk). A2 = think-aloud usability with own use of the app (n = 8-12, iterative rounds) + SUS-BR.
- Population: UNIPAC adult students (>=18), any course, Portuguese speakers; exclude Psychology/Medicine students from A1 primary analysis or stratify (domain knowledge).
- Sample: A1 primary = proportion correct on the key item "Este resultado significa que a pessoa tem TDAH?" (correct = Não). Precision target: n=100 per version gives +/-.06 at p=.90; superiority .75 -> .90 needs 100/group -> total 200 (4 cells x 50). Minimal version: single-arm n=100 (current report only) to estimate comprehension with +/-6-8%.
- Procedure: (1) Recruitment by poster/e-mail by people who don't grade the participants; (2) online TCLE (registro eletrônico; 510 Art. 15); (3) app (CS) randomizes version x vignette, shows the report screen exactly as rendered in-app; (4) 8-10 comprehension items developed by psych students, content-validated by 5-7 judges (CVI); (5) perceived certainty, intended action, distress VAS 0-10 before/after; (6) SUS-BR (Lourenço 2022) on app screenshots/flow; (7) debrief + general mental-health resource sheet given to everyone.
- Outcomes: primary = % correct on "not a diagnosis" item; secondary = total comprehension score, "EEG não altera o resultado" item, correct next-step item, distress change, SUS.
- Preregistered hypotheses: H1 >=80% correct on the primary item with current report (lower 95% CI bound >= .70); H2 improved report > current (OR > 1); H3 no increase in distress VAS for the positive vignette with improved vs current (non-inferiority margin 1 point).
- Analysis: Wilson CIs; logistic regression correct ~ version * vignette + course; linear model for distress; SUS mean with 95% CI; thematic analysis of think-aloud (2 coders, kappa).
- Ethics: CNS 510/2016, risk "mínimo" (A1 uses fictional data); A2 uses own ASRS -> referral sheet + supervisor on call.
- Risks: ceiling effect; participants self-diagnosing anyway; social-desirability. Mitigate with forced-choice + confidence items.
- CS owns: report UI variants, randomization, logging (time on screen, taps), export. Psych owns: item writing + CVI, vignette script, TCLE, CEP submission, think-aloud sessions, qualitative coding.

### STUDY B — Test-retest reliability of BrainLink-derived EEG features in adults (moderate; ~1-2 semesters)
- RQ: How reliable (ICC, SEM, SDC, Bland-Altman LoA) are BrainLink-derived features (EC/EO alpha reactivity, IAF, aperiodic exponent & offset, relative band power, blink rate, % usable signal) across (i) re-application within session and (ii) one week?
- Design: repeated-measures reliability study; 3 recordings: S1a, S1b (remove + reapply headset, 10 min later), S2 (7 +/- 2 days, same time of day +/- 1 h). Optionally extend app protocol to 2 min EO + 2 min EC to test whether 1 min suffices (compare ICC of first 60 s vs full 120 s).
- Population: healthy-adult convenience sample 18-40 (students/staff); record caffeine, sleep hours, medication (incl. stimulants), handedness, contact lenses, hair/skin prep. No ADHD inclusion criterion; ASRS collected only as descriptive covariate (optional).
- Sample size: H0 rho=.50 vs H1 rho=.75 -> 36 (2 sessions) or 24 (3 recordings) (Walter 1998; verified by simulation). Precision: rho .75, CI width .30 -> 34 (k=2) (Bonett 2002). Target 40 completers; recruit 50 (20% loss for bad signal/no-show).
- Procedure: standardized room, seated, fixation cross on phone held in stand at fixed distance, skin cleaned at electrode site; app records raw EEG + quality index; psych student follows a written SOP checklist (identical script) and logs deviations; blink validation subset (n=10) with simultaneous video for manual blink count.
- Feature definitions frozen before data (preregistered): Welch PSD (2-s Hann, 50% overlap); alpha 8-13 Hz; reactivity = log10(alpha EC / alpha EO); IAF = peak in 7-13 Hz of EC spectrum (or centre of gravity; report both, "no peak" as missing); aperiodic exponent/offset by specparam fixed mode 2-40 Hz (report R^2; exclude fits R^2 < .9 — threshold to preregister); blink rate per minute in EO via threshold on 0.5-10 Hz filtered signal, validated vs video; % epochs passing quality.
- Outcomes & hypotheses: H1 EC alpha relative power ICC(A,1) lower 95% CI bound > .50 (prior: Rogers 2016 NeuroSky FP1 EC ICC .76-.85); H2 alpha reactivity ICC > .50; H3 (exploratory, two-sided) IAF and aperiodic exponent ICC — expect moderate (McKeown 2024 research EEG: exponent ~.70; forehead single dry channel likely lower); H4 blink detection vs video: Bland-Altman bias within +/-2 blinks/min (threshold to preregister).
- Analysis: ICC two-way mixed, absolute agreement, single measure (state model/type/definition, software — Koo & Li 2016); 95% CI-based labels (<.5 poor, .5-.75 moderate, .75-.9 good, >.9 excellent); SEM = SD*sqrt(1-ICC); SDC95 = 1.96*sqrt(2)*SEM; Bland-Altman with log-transform for power; variance decomposition (subject / placement / day) via linear mixed model; sensitivity analyses excluding caffeine/sleep outliers.
- Ethics: CNS 466/2012 (non-invasive biosignal; risk mínimo: skin irritation, discomfort); TCLE in 2 vias; no individual EEG interpretation given back.
- Risks: dry-electrode/Bluetooth data loss; posterior alpha poorly captured at forehead -> IAF may be unreliable (that itself is a publishable finding); ocular artefacts dominate Fp sites.
- CS owns: raw data capture, timestamps, quality metric, signal processing pipeline (open code, version-tagged), blink detector, export. Psych owns: SOP, participant handling, covariate questionnaires, data-quality logs, reliability statistics (with CS), write-up of measurement-error implications.
- Value: prerequisite for ANY later claim about BrainLink features; publishable as a methods/reliability note.

### STUDY C — Within-person state study: smartphone SART with thought probes + frontal EEG/blinks, related to ASRS and MW questionnaires (most ambitious; 2 semesters)
- RQ: (1) Within persons, do periods preceding self-reported mind wandering / vigilance decrement show higher RT variability, higher blink rate and altered frontal spectral power than on-task periods? (2) Between persons, do ASRS-6 scores relate to probe-caught spontaneous MW, RT variability (tau), MWQ-BR and MEWS-BR?
- Design: single-session experimental-correlational, within-subject; ~18-min SART on the phone (Arabacı & Parris 2018 template: digits 1-9, 250 ms + 900 ms mask, no-go = 3; 900 trials; ~20-24 probes, randomly 1 per ~40-45 trials) with 2-part probe: (a) on task / intentional MW / unintentional MW (Seli/Arabacı), (b) "senti que minha atenção estava fora da tarefa mesmo sem lembrar o conteúdo?" (Yeo 2026). Resting 1 min EO/EC before and after (also gives retest data for Study B features).
- Population: adult UNIPAC students 18-35, unselected for ADHD (continuum approach); record self-reported prior ADHD dx and medication as covariates (no diagnostic grouping claims).
- Sample size: between-person r = .30 -> 85 (Arabacı & Parris r = .38 inattention-spontaneous MW); within-person paired d_z = .40 -> 52. Target 90 completers; recruit 105 (15% EEG loss).
- Procedure: TCLE -> ASRS-6 (app) -> MWQ-BR (Peloso 2024) + MEWS-BR (Figueiredo 2018; permission needed) + sleep/caffeine items -> headset fit + quality check -> rest EO/EC -> SART practice (18 trials + 2 probes) -> SART -> rest EO/EC -> debrief, ASRS feedback per Study A best-practice report + referral sheet to all.
- Outcomes: behavioural: commission errors, RT CV, ex-Gaussian tau (Bella-Fernández 2024: tau largest for sustained attention, d .62), probe responses. EEG (10-s window before each probe, and per 3-min block): blink rate, frontal theta (4-7), alpha (8-13), beta (13-30) relative power, aperiodic exponent (exploratory).
- Preregistered hypotheses: H1 MW reports increase over blocks (manipulation check; Zanesco 2024); H2 RT CV higher in 8 trials before MW vs on-task probes; H3 blink rate higher before MW vs on-task (Riby 2025); H4 two-sided/exploratory: frontal theta/alpha differ pre-MW vs on-task (Kam 2022: spectral findings less robust); H5 ASRS-6 total correlates positively with proportion unintentional MW (r > 0) and with MWQ-BR/MEWS-BR; H6 exploratory: ASRS ~ tau; ASRS ~ EEG/blink markers (expect small/null; Unsworth 2019).
- Analysis: multilevel logistic (probe response ~ block + (1 + block | subject)); multilevel linear (pre-probe feature ~ probe state + block + (1|subject)); Pearson/Spearman with 95% CI for between-person; Holm correction across H2-H4; equivalence tests (TOST, bounds r = +/-.20) for H6 nulls; preregister on OSF before data collection; share code.
- Ethics: CNS 466/2012 (biosignal) + ASRS screening -> CNS 466 III.2(o) referral plan; psychologist supervision per CFP recommendation; Plataforma Brasil; LGPD (pseudonymized IDs, separate key file).
- Risks: phone-based timing jitter (CS must measure input latency; use same phone model for all); probe reactivity; EEG/task clock sync; fatigue; low variance in ASRS in a convenience sample; temptation to over-interpret EEG as "TDAH biomarker" — reports must say "correlates of attentional state in this sample".
- CS owns: SART implementation with ms timestamps, probe UI, EEG-event synchronization, latency test, feature extraction, data pipeline. Psych owns: task parameter justification, probe wording (Portuguese, pilot with cognitive interviews), questionnaire battery permissions, administration SOP, preregistration of hypotheses, multilevel analysis (with CS), debrief/referral.

### Sequencing recommendation
A (semester 1, also yields the final report wording) -> B (semester 1-2; pilot also tunes SOP and quality thresholds) -> C (semester 2-3; uses B's reliability to choose which EEG features are worth testing). Single umbrella CEP protocol under the supervising professor with later amendments (CNS 510 Art. 27 allows student sub-projects as amendments if objectives/methods are not essentially changed) — or two protocols (A under 510; B+C under 466).
